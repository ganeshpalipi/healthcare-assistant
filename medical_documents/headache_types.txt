# Headache Types and Management

## Overview
Headaches are one of the most common medical complaints. Most headaches are not serious and can be managed with lifestyle changes and over-the-counter medications. However, some headaches may indicate a more serious condition.

## Types of Headaches

### 1. Tension-Type Headache
The most common type of headache. It feels like a constant ache or pressure around the head, especially at the temples or back of the head and neck.

**Characteristics:**
- Mild to moderate pain
- Pressing or tightening feeling (not pulsating)
- Both sides of the head affected
- May last from 30 minutes to several days

**Common Triggers:**
- Stress
- Poor posture
- Fatigue
- Eye strain
- Dehydration

### 2. Migraine
A type of primary headache that causes intense, throbbing pain, usually on one side of the head.

**Characteristics:**
- Moderate to severe pain (often pulsating or throbbing)
- Usually on one side of the head
- May be accompanied by nausea, vomiting
- Sensitivity to light, sound, or smell
- May last from 4 to 72 hours
- May have aura (visual disturbances) before the headache

**Common Triggers:**
- Hormonal changes in women
- Certain foods and food additives
- Stress
- Sleep changes
- Physical exertion
- Changes in weather or barometric pressure

### 3. Cluster Headache
One of the most painful types of headaches. They occur in cyclical patterns or clusters.

**Characteristics:**
- Extremely severe pain (often described as piercing or burning)
- Usually around or behind one eye
- May be accompanied by eye watering, nasal congestion, or swelling
- Individual attacks last 15 minutes to 3 hours
- Often occur at the same time each day

### 4. Sinus Headache
Pain caused by inflammation or congestion in the sinus cavities.

**Characteristics:**
- Deep, constant pain in the cheekbones, forehead, or bridge of the nose
- Worsens with sudden head movement or straining
- Accompanied by other sinus symptoms (nasal discharge, feeling of fullness in ears, facial swelling)

## When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

Seek emergency care if your headache:
- Is sudden and severe (thunderclap headache)
- Is accompanied by fever, stiff neck, confusion, seizures, double vision, weakness, numbness, or difficulty speaking
- Is accompanied by a head injury
- Worsens after a head injury
- Is new and occurs after age 50

## General Management
- Rest in a quiet, dark room
- Stay hydrated
- Over-the-counter pain relievers may help (follow package instructions)
- Apply a cold or warm compress to the head or neck
- Practice relaxation techniques
- Maintain regular sleep patterns

Note: This information is for educational purposes only and does not replace professional medical advice. Always consult a healthcare provider for persistent or severe headaches.
