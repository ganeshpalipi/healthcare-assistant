# Diabetes Mellitus - Overview

## What is Diabetes?
Diabetes mellitus is a metabolic disorder characterized by chronically high blood sugar (glucose) levels. It occurs when the body either does not produce enough insulin or cannot effectively use the insulin it produces.

## Types of Diabetes

### Type 1 Diabetes
- Autoimmune condition where the body's immune system destroys insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas
- Usually diagnosed in children and young adults
- Requires lifelong insulin therapy
- Accounts for about 5-10% of diabetes cases

### Type 2 Diabetes
- Most common form (90-95% of cases)
- Body becomes resistant to insulin or doesn't produce enough
- Often associated with lifestyle factors
- Can sometimes be managed with diet, exercise, and oral medications
- Risk factors include obesity, physical inactivity, family history

### Gestational Diabetes
- Develops during pregnancy
- Usually resolves after childbirth
- Increases risk of developing Type 2 diabetes later

## Common Symptoms
- Increased thirst (polydipsia)
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Increased hunger (polyphagia)
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing wounds
- Unexplained weight loss (Type 1)
- Tingling or numbness in hands/feet

## Complications (Long-term)
- Cardiovascular disease
- Kidney damage (nephropathy)
- Eye damage (retinopathy)
- Nerve damage (neuropathy)
- Foot damage
- Skin conditions
- Hearing impairment
- Alzheimer's disease risk

## General Management
- Regular blood sugar monitoring
- Healthy eating (balanced diet, portion control)
- Regular physical activity
- Medication as prescribed by a doctor
- Regular check-ups with healthcare provider
- Foot care
- Eye examinations

## Prevention (Type 2)
- Maintain healthy body weight
- Regular physical activity (at least 150 minutes/week moderate activity)
- Healthy diet rich in whole grains, fruits, vegetables
- Limit sugary beverages and refined carbohydrates

Note: This information is for educational purposes only and does not replace professional medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for diabetes management.
