# Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) - Overview

## What is Hypertension?
Hypertension, commonly known as high blood pressure, is a chronic medical condition in which the blood pressure in the arteries is persistently elevated. It is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease.

## Blood Pressure Categories
- Normal: Less than 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: Systolic 120-129 and Diastolic less than 80
- Stage 1 Hypertension: Systolic 130-139 or Diastolic 80-89
- Stage 2 Hypertension: Systolic 140 or higher or Diastolic 90 or higher
- Hypertensive Crisis: Systolic over 180 and/or Diastolic over 120

## Risk Factors
- Family history of hypertension
- Age (risk increases with age)
- Obesity or overweight
- Physical inactivity
- High sodium (salt) intake
- Low potassium intake
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Tobacco use
- Stress
- Chronic conditions (diabetes, kidney disease, sleep apnea)

## Symptoms
Hypertension is often called the "silent killer" because it typically has no symptoms. Some people may experience:
- Headaches (especially in the morning)
- Shortness of breath
- Nosebleeds
- Dizziness
- Chest pain
- Visual changes

## Complications
- Heart attack
- Stroke
- Heart failure
- Kidney disease
- Vision loss
- Peripheral artery disease
- Aneurysm
- Metabolic syndrome

## General Management
- DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)
- Reduce sodium intake (less than 2,300mg/day, ideally 1,500mg/day)
- Regular physical activity (150 min/week moderate aerobic)
- Maintain healthy weight
- Limit alcohol
- Quit smoking
- Stress management
- Medication as prescribed
- Regular blood pressure monitoring

## When to Seek Emergency Care
Seek immediate medical attention if blood pressure is 180/120 mmHg or higher and accompanied by:
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Numbness or weakness
- Difficulty speaking
- Severe headache
- Vision problems
